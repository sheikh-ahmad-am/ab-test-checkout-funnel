We redesigned the checkout flow. But did it actually work? I ran the full analysis on twenty thousand users, half on the old checkout, half on the new one.

The new flow lifted purchases from eleven point nine eight percent to fourteen point five percent: a gain of two point five two percentage points, with a ninety-five percent confidence interval from plus one point five eight to plus three point four five, and a p-value near zero. Verdict: ship it.

But segmentation told a sharper story. Desktop gained seven percentage points; mobile was flat and not significant. So the real recommendation: ship to desktop, hold mobile back for its own diagnosis. The funnel confirmed the lift landed exactly where a checkout redesign should: between checkout started and payment done, up eight point zero six points.

I also compared first-touch versus last-touch attribution: social wins discovery, paid wins closing. Eight interview terms, one evidence-backed decision.
